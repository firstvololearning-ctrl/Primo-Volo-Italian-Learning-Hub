PILC Elementary Italian · Scope & Sequence 2025–2026 + Curriculum Update

Connecting the required curriculum, Primo Volo practice, and the class text

This planning crosswalk is for classes using the PILC Elementary Italian Scope and Sequence together with Primo Volo and the Ambarabà text. The three resources do not follow an identical order. The connections below identify useful overlap in vocabulary, communicative functions, and recurring language structures. This crosswalk shows connections and possible reinforcement; it is not the canonical inventory of the current digital Primo Volo app.

1 · PILC Scope & Sequence Establishes the required themes and curricular focus across the school year.
2 · Primo Volo Provides visual, oral, interactive, and printable preparation and reinforcement within those themes.
3 · Connection to the Text Identifies places where related language appears in Ambarabà and can be understood, used, or extended in a richer context.
Important: a connection does not mean that a PILC month and an Ambarabà unit are identical. Some connections are direct; others are useful linguistic bridges. Where the overlap is limited, it is labeled that way.

Elementare 3

The priority changes here. Primo Volo can help make enough high-frequency vocabulary, directions, questions, and language patterns familiar that students can access increasingly demanding Ambarabà 3 tasks.

PROVIDE ACCESS

The goal is not to preteach the Ambarabà lesson. The goal is to reduce avoidable language barriers so students can use their attention for the language and grammar that are genuinely new in the text.

Needs to be familiar Primo Volo preparation Connection to the Text Where in Ambarabà 3 Why it improves access
Common task directions
ascolta, guarda, leggi, scrivi, completa, abbina, scegli, cerchia, colora 		Repeated classroom expressions, visual direction cues, and predictable activity routines. These directions recur throughout the student text and workbook. Across Ambarabà 3 Students can begin the actual language task without first decoding what they are being asked to do.
Numbers and tens
especially 10, 20, 30…100 		Recognition, listening, matching, and increasingly quick retrieval with the current digital numbers 1–20. Treat 30–100 as PILC/text extension language. Numbers and tens appear immediately and support the clock work that follows. Modulo 1 · Unità 1 · Non ho tempo
Workbook begins with numbers/tens 		Students do not have to relearn number words while simultaneously learning to tell time.
Clock language
Che ore sono? · Sono le… · e un quarto · e mezza · meno un quarto 		Visual clocks, listening, Q&A practice, recognition before independent responses. Clock time is central to the first unit. Modulo 1 · Unità 1 · Non ho tempo
Student text pp. 6–13; workbook pp. 4–20 		Time becomes usable context for routines instead of a second unfamiliar system students must decode.
Daily routine vocabulary
mi sveglio, mi alzo, mi lavo, mi vesto, faccio colazione, vado a scuola, pranzo, faccio i compiti, gioco, ceno, vado a letto 		Picture → phrase → sequencing → supported sentence → retrieval with the current digital routines: Mi sveglio, Mi alzo, Faccio colazione, Vado a scuola, Torno a casa, Faccio i compiti, Gioco, Leggo, Vado a dormire, Dormo. The additional routines in the first column are PILC/text extensions. The first unit requires students to understand and talk about routines, including reflexive actions. Modulo 1 · Unità 1 · Non ho tempo Students can focus on time, verb forms, and sentence relationships rather than learning every routine word at once.
High-frequency first-person verbs
vado, faccio, mangio, bevo, gioco 		Reusable first-person sentence frames across familiar topics. Ambarabà 3 begins varying subjects and verb forms beyond familiar first-person language. Non ho tempo and later units A stable first-person anchor makes it easier to notice what changes in other forms.
Days, months & seasons
plus basic Quando? language 		Calendar recognition, sequencing, short questions and responses. Months, seasons, festivities and chronological time become explicit targets. Modulo 1 · Unità 2 · Quando? Familiar calendar vocabulary frees attention for questions, rules, and new verb structures.
Weather language
Che tempo fa? · Piove · Nevica · C'è il sole · C'è vento · C'è la nebbia 		Visual weather vocabulary, listening, and short Q&A exchanges. Current digital weather includes sun, wind, rain, snow, cloud, heat, cold, and storms; fog is a connected PILC/text extension. Weather phenomena are an explicit unit focus. Modulo 1 · Unità 3 · Che tempo fa? The text can extend familiar weather chunks instead of introducing the entire semantic field from scratch.
Feelings & sensations
sto bene/male · sono felice/triste · ho caldo/freddo 		Visual identification, listening, personal responses, and short frames. Weather is linked to sensations and emotional states in the same unit. Modulo 1 · Unità 3 · Che tempo fa? Students can understand the relationship between weather and how someone feels.
Core question language
Che cosa fai? · Dove? · Quando? · A che ora? · Con chi? · Com'è? · Perché? 		Repeated partner Q&A with predictable question frames and meaningful answers. Question forms recur across the Ambarabà 3 syllabus and actual Unit 1 activities. Across Modules 1–3 Understanding the question itself becomes automatic enough to focus on formulating the response.
Preferences
Mi piace… · Non mi piace… 		Preference choices, partner questions, and personal responses across topics. Preference language is treated as recurring language and used in richer contexts. Modulo 2 · Unità 2 · Al circo A familiar high-utility chunk can support description and interaction rather than becoming another new structure.
C'è / Ci sono Picture/scene description with singular and plural examples. The structures recur in descriptions of places and environments. Al parco and Dove? Students can use a familiar description frame while attending to new vocabulary and context.
Article + noun as a unit
and basic adjective agreement 		Learn nouns with their articles; compare familiar descriptive pairs such as masculine/feminine and singular/plural forms. Ambarabà 3 explicitly develops animal description and noun–adjective agreement. Modulo 2 · Unità 3 · Al museo di scienze Familiar noun/article combinations make later agreement patterns easier to notice and learn.
Partner Q&A routine Ask → listen → answer → switch roles through short supported exchanges. The text explicitly asks students to work with partners and alternate asking and answering questions. Example in Non ho tempo, student text p. 13 The interactional routine itself is familiar, leaving more attention available for the Italian being practiced.

What Primo Volo does not need to preteach

The goal is not to teach every Ambarabà 3 grammar target in advance. More complex verb paradigms, expanded reflexive forms, possessives, modal verbs, relative structures, passato prossimo, and other new grammar can remain instructional content of the text. Primo Volo's access role is to make the high-frequency vocabulary, directions, questions, and foundational language patterns familiar enough that students have room to learn what is new.
