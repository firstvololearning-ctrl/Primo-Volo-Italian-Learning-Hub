A quick guide to using Primo Volo

✈️ Primo Volo · Home

Flexible curriculum support

Start with your teaching goal. Use the pieces that help.

Primo Volo connects vocabulary, useful language, printables, culture, and optional online practice for novice Italian learners. You do not need to use every activity or follow one fixed route.

A simple teaching arc is: understand → practice with support → build and retrieve language → use it with increasing independence.

💻 Online practice is optional 🧭 Flexible starting points and supports

This guide focuses on how to use Primo Volo instructionally. Planning, teaching materials, and assessment resources are organized in the Educators & Families menu on the home page.

🚀 Start here Choose the target, then choose the level of support. ▼

Begin with the language you want students to understand or use. If a Starting Check is available, this optional digital activity can show what a learner already recognizes before practice. Otherwise, begin where your instruction and observation suggest.

Need more support? Use clear visuals, Italian audio, modeled language, recognition tasks, and familiar sentence frames.
Ready for less support? Move toward retrieval, sentence building, partner interaction, writing, and more independent use.
Students can move forward or return to an earlier support. The sequence is instructional support, not a lock-step requirement.
✈️ How the language builds Keep the progression in mind without requiring every activity. ▼

Impara · Learn can introduce vocabulary, useful language, and selected language patterns with visuals, audio, and examples. Later activities reduce support through choices, structured sentence building, partial spelling cues, and independent responses.

💬 Teaching with useful language Connect vocabulary to sentences, interaction, and grammar in context. ▼

New vocabulary becomes more useful when students repeatedly meet it inside familiar questions, responses, and sentence frames.

Io vedo… Io ho… Mi piace… Non mi piace… È… C’è…
For interaction Use partner games and Chat Mats to model a question and response, then reduce visual or verbal support as students become more comfortable.
For grammar Model recurring patterns, notice them aloud, compare forms, and teach them explicitly when useful within the language students are already using.

Grammar opportunities may include articles and noun patterns, gender and number, agreement, recurring verb forms, word order, and question-and-answer structures.

Looking for the language for a specific topic? Open Curriculum Overview & Scope to see the vocabulary, target language, and useful phrases currently included for each topic. Find it under Educators & Families → Planning on the Primo Volo home page.
📊 Checks, progress, and what the data mean Use the information to guide practice, not to label mastery. ▼
Starting Checks The first check provides a starting-point snapshot; later Check-ins can help show change over time. Results stay separate from ordinary practice, mastery, and Italy Journey progress.
Practice Progress Shows activities practiced within a topic. Completion means practice coverage, not mastery.
Italy Journey Turns completed practice into a motivational route through Italy. It is a completion and engagement tool, not an accuracy score.
Progress + Suggested Next Practice Progress keeps scored practice information and optional Check-in history separate. Suggested Next Practice uses practice data to offer a possible next step.

Adaptive Starting Checks may stop after recognition when production would not change the recommendation. When production is useful, learners try only targets they recognized. Some Starting Checks also include a distinct Useful Language task. That result is reported separately and does not change the core vocabulary recognition and production recommendation. Suggested activities are guidance, not locked pathways.

These tools guide instruction and practice. They are not grades, formal placement tests, mastery decisions, or ACTFL proficiency ratings.
